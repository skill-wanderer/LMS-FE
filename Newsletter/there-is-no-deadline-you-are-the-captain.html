There Is No Deadline: You Are the Captain

Lesson 6 of How to Become Your Own Teacher: Learning How to Learn is finished. Read it, watch it, listen to it, page through the slides, or see the whole lesson on one page.

Learning has no exam date unless you set one. School is a scheduled liner. It sails on a fixed date, follows a route somebody else charted, and bends for no single passenger. Teaching yourself is a small ship with you at the wheel, and nobody is standing at the other end with a stopwatch.

That is not permission to drift. A captain still picks the next port, but a waypoint you set yourself is a tool: when the weather turns, you move it, and moving it is a decision rather than a defeat. What replaces the timetable is navigation, a loop you keep running:
The navigation loop
1

Learn

Pick up something new.
2

Use

Put it to work on something real.
3

Take a bearing

Find out what it was actually worth, which is often not what you expected.
4

Adjust

Keep what is useful, throw the rest overboard, and set the next heading.
↻  Then back to 1, from where you now know you are.

A timetable has to guess the weather months in advance. A navigator can look out of the window.

The lesson also covers the one question that tells you what to throw overboard, why changing course is seamanship and not shipwreck, and the four hazards every voyage meets: storms, setbacks, fog, and voices from the shore. None of them means the voyage has gone wrong. They are the adventure.

A ship making two knots in the right direction will arrive. A fast ship at anchor will not.
