Nobody Is Coming to Teach You (And That Is Fine)

Lesson 4 of How to Become Your Own Teacher: Learning How to Learn is finished. Read it, watch it, listen to it, page through the slides, or see the whole lesson on one page.

The fastest way to learn something is usually to have an expert sitting next to you. This lesson does not pretend otherwise. If you can afford one, hire one.

But be precise about what you are buying. It is not the material, which is in books, documentation and videos, most of it free. You are buying judgement about the material. It comes in three parts, and each one can be rebuilt another way.
 

Compression

An expert has already read the twenty things you would have to read, and can tell you which three matter.

Instead: a well-made course or book is that filtering, bought secondhand. Check it against other independent sources.
 

Correction

They see your mistake while it is small, before you spend six months building on top of it.

Instead: build something real and let reality correct you. It is slower than a mentor, but it is free, and it never flatters you.
 

Pruning

They tell you what to ignore, and wave you away from a dead end in a single sentence.

Instead: find the communities where practitioners argue with each other, and learn which questions are worth arguing about.

None of these is as good as an expert at your elbow. Together they are good enough, and they are available to you today.

The lesson also covers the resource most self-learners never use: experienced people give their judgement away for free, to anyone who asks well. It walks through how to ask, the three real costs of teaching yourself, and the two things you gain that a taught student rarely does.

What makes self-teaching valuable is not that it is free. It is that it never runs out.
