Summary and Takeaway

Lesson 7 of How to Become Your Own Teacher: Learning How to Learn is finished, and with it, Module 1 is complete. Read it, watch it, listen to it, page through the slides, or see the whole lesson on one page.

Six lessons, one argument: the ability to teach yourself is not a supplement to your education. It is the part that keeps working after the education stops.

Lesson 7 looks back over the whole chart before you set out on your own. Here is the route so far, one waypoint per lesson:
Module 1 in six waypoints
1

Take the teacher's job

Deciding what to learn, choosing the material, checking your understanding, and keeping yourself going.
2

A floor, not a ceiling

Formal education gives you foundations, a credential, structure, and peers, built for an average learner who does not exist.
3

Relearning is the skill

Principles last decades, practices last years, tools last months. A diploma is a snapshot, and relearning renews it.
4

Nobody owes you a lesson

And that is survivable. Primary sources, real projects, and communities of practitioners rebuild what an expert sells.
5

Mix your methods

Every method has its own way of lying to you, so combine several and let their blind spots cancel out.
6

No deadline but your own

Swap the timetable for navigation: learn, use, take a bearing, adjust.
✦  Destination: a heading of your own, and a first day.

Strip the six lessons down and they turn out to be one observation seen from six angles. Every limitation of formal education traces back to the cost of serving everyone. Every advantage of teaching yourself traces back to serving exactly one person.

Formal education hires one teacher for thirty people. Becoming your own teacher puts one teacher on one student, and you are both of them.

The lesson also explains why the module deliberately gave you no technique, and names the two ways to get all of this wrong: over-correcting until school looks worthless, and agreeing with everything while changing nothing. Then it leaves you with one question, not a checklist:

What is one thing you have wanted to learn, and have been waiting for a course, a school, or a person to hand to you?
