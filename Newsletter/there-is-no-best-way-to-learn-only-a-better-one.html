There Is No Best Way to Learn, Only a Better One

Lesson 8 of How to Become Your Own Teacher: Learning How to Learn is finished, and it opens Module 2, Many Ways to Learn. Read it, watch it, listen to it, page through the slides, or see the whole lesson on one page.

Ask ten people how they learn best, and you will get ten confident answers. The watchers swear by videos and courses, the builders by making things, the readers by books, and the talkers by people who do the work.

Every one of those methods genuinely works, and nobody in those camps is lying to you. Where they go wrong is the step after: turning what worked in one subject, at one point in their life, into a rule for everyone and everything. So Lesson 8 trades the usual question for a shorter one:

What is the best way to learn?

Better for what?

Nobody calls a boat a bad vehicle because it failed to climb a mountain. It was simply the wrong choice for the ground.
 
Same learner, three fields
 

Marketing

Reading books and newsletters, and listening to podcasts

The core is people, who change slowly, so the best thinking about them is in books. Channels change every quarter, and newsletters and podcasts keep you current. A real campaign costs money and weeks, which makes building an expensive teacher here.
 
 

Tech

Watching courses and videos, then making something

So much of it happens on a screen that a video can show you exactly what to type and where to click. Trying it yourself takes minutes, costs nothing, and either works or it does not.
 
 

Entrepreneurship

Trial and error, and talking with people in the field

Much of it is never written down, because it belongs to one market, one customer, one moment. Someone two steps ahead can tell you in ten minutes what would take you a year to find out.
 
Go to where the knowledge lives, and let the subject choose your method before your habits do.

Those three rows are the story at the heart of the lesson. Same person, same habits, same brain, and three different answers. There is no best way to learn. There is only a better one, for one specific situation.

Two more things shape the answer. What you need: knowing about something, knowing how to do it, and knowing when to do it are three different kinds of knowledge, and no single method gives you all three. And how far along you are: a book or a course hands a beginner the map, while building, trial and error, and conversation teach faster later on.

The lesson also heads off a common mix-up. This is not the “visual learner” idea, which has not held up when researchers tested it properly. Your preferences decide what you will keep doing. The subject decides what will actually teach you.

It ends with a two-minute exercise. Name the method you would recommend to a friend, pick one thing you are learning right now, and ask yourself:

Is my default the better method for this, or just the one that worked last time?
